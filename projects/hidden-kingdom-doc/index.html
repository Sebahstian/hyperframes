The Hidden Kingdom

A survey of three contradictions

Act One
The Regnal List

Nine kings are named between the Founding and the Ruin. Summed from the coronation tablets, their reigns cover four hundred years. The chronicle sells us three hundred. The overrun sits entirely inside the sixth reign.

One century is missing from the count

“And in the ninth year of the sixth king,a hundred winters unnumbered, the sky was quiet.”

Act Two
The Core Sample

Nine metres beneath the crater floor the survey drill passed through sterile rock, and then through cut stone. Dressed masonry, laid by hand, three centuries after the city above it was said to have burned.

“Built upon the bedrockbefore the sky fell, surviving three successive eras.”

Act Three
The Blade
Item Inspection#212 / RELIC

The relic is dated to the First Age by every catalogue that lists it. Its own inscription credits a smithing house the tax rolls do not record for another two hundred years.

“Struck by the House of Ardenmoor,keepers of the first flame, for a king long buried.”

The smith outlived the kingdom
Three records. One window.

Three independent records, each wrong in a different way, each wrong about the same stretch of time — a century the chronicle never describes, bracketed on both sides by events it dates precisely.

The kingdom did not fall in the First Age.

It fell three hundred years later, in a century no one wrote down.
